# Adversarial Design Review — subc Federation Phase 3 (DRAFT v1)

Reviewed both `docs/subc-federation-phase3-design.md` (DRAFT v1) and `docs/subc-federation-design.md` (v4.1). Inheritance claims verified against phase-2 text via direct grep of the phase-2 doc. Codebase confirms the fed-module does not yet exist (greenfield); `revoked_by_account` and `pipe_token` appear only in the phase-3 doc.

## Finding 1: Candidate-racing dial model is NEW, not inherited — "pubkey tie-break as phase 2" is a false inheritance claim
- **Severity**: HIGH
- **Section**: 5.4 (line 163),  (inheritance preamble)
- **Confidence**: high
- **Problem**: Phase-3 5.4 states "the initiator (pubkey tie-break as phase 2) tries candidates in order: lan → public → relay, first-success-wins." This presents symmetric candidate racing as inherited from phase-2. It is NOT. Phase-2's Fork 3 model is explicitly asymmetric: "reachable peer is server, NAT'd peer holds the outbound tunnel, relay when neither is reachable" (phase-2 line 242). Phase-2 has NO concept of "candidate", "dial", "first-success-wins", or "pubkey tie-break for initiator selection" — grep for `tie-break|candidate|dial|first-success|simultaneous` in the phase-2 doc returns zero relevant hits. The entire racing dial model is phase-3 invention. The danger: presenting new mechanics as inherited means the council gate may not scrutinize the racing-correctness surface (double-win, tie-break under simultaneous dial, stale-candidate races) with the rigor a new mechanism demands.
- **Inheritance verdict**: phase-2 does NOT support this claim.
- **Concrete fix**: Strike "as phase 2" from 5.4. Specify the tie-break as a NEW phase-3 rule: e.g. "the device whose pubkey sorts lower is the initiator; both may still dial candidates, but only the initiator's `connect_request` triggers `relay_open`." Document the double-win resolution (Finding 2).

## Finding 2: Double-win — TCP session and WS session both establishing simultaneously is unresolved
- **Severity**: HIGH
- **Section**: 5.4, 
- **Confidence**: high
- **Problem**: 5.4 says "both sides then race candidates (" with first-success-wins per side. With lan→public→relay ordering and ~2s per-candidate timeout, it is entirely possible for side A to win the LAN candidate (TCP session establishing) while side B, whose LAN candidate is asymmetrically unreachable, simultaneously wins the relay candidate (WS session establishing) — both Noise handshakes in flight to the same peer. Nothing specifies what resolves a double-win: does the later-arriving handshake get torn down? Does the ledger see two sessions to the same peer (which one owns the effect_id high-water)? Phase-2's one-loopback-per-(peer,module) model assumes ONE session per peer; two concurrent sessions break that invariant. The "winning transport is remembered per peer" (line 168) presumes a single winner.
- **Concrete fix**: Specify a deterministic single-winner rule: (a) both sides attempt candidates but only the tie-break-loser accepts inbound; the tie-break-winner tears down any session it initiated if it receives an inbound from the loser; OR (b) after first-success, broadcast a `dial_won {carrier, nonce}` over signaling and the loser tears down. Require that at most one Noise session per peer-pair is ever admitted to the ledger; a second handshake to an already-connected peer is refused with a typed error.

## Finding 3: "carrier-blind / relay-path partitions classify exactly like TCP partitions" is an unproven assertion — phase-2 never had a second carrier
- **Severity**: HIGH
- **Section**:  (lines 193-195)
- **Confidence**: high
- **Problem**:  claims "Everything above the carrier (Noise, strict JSON, effect ledger, forwarder, keepalive/reaping) is carrier-blind. Keepalive cadence and 3× reap window unchanged; relay-path partitions classify exactly like TCP partitions." Grep for `carrier|carrier-blind|transport-agnostic|TCP partition|relay-path` in the phase-2 doc returns ZERO hits. Phase-2 had exactly one carrier (direct TCP/Noise). "Carrier-blind" is therefore not an inherited property — it is a NEW claim about a NEW carrier that has never been tested. The reap classifier (phase-2 6.2: "keepalive reaper closes per-peer loopback connections") was designed against TCP semantics (clean FIN, RST, or silent drop). A relay pipe has a DIFFERENT failure mode: RelayDO idle-teardown is a server-side decision that tears down both WS legs — this looks identical to a peer silent-drop from the reaper's viewpoint, but the peer is NOT partitioned, it just needs a fresh grant. This can produce false-positive partitions → spurious GOODBYE → `OutcomeUnknown` for in-flight effects that were actually delivered.
- **Inheritance verdict**: phase-2 does NOT support this claim.
- **Concrete fix**: Do not assert equivalence. Specify the relay-path partition classifier explicitly: (a) distinguish "relay pipe torn down by idle timeout" (recoverable, reconnect via fresh grant, NOT a partition) from "peer missed keepalive across a fresh-grant reconnect window" (true partition); (b) define the reap window for the relay path as `keepalive_cadence × 3 + reconnect_grant_latency_budget`, not the bare TCP 3× window; (c) add a golden test: relay idle-teardown mid-call must NOT produce GOODBYE if the peer reconnects within the budget.

## Finding 4: "Noise session resumption = ordinary re-handshake, cheap" is unsupported and interacts with the incarnation ledger
- **Severity**: MEDIUM
- **Section**:  (line 204)
- **Confidence**: high
- **Problem**:  claims relay idle-teardown reconnect is "Noise session resumption = ordinary re-handshake, cheap." Grep for `session resumption|re-handshake|resumption` in phase-2 returns ZERO hits. Phase-2 has no session-resumption concept. Two problems: (1) "cheap" is hand-waved — a full Noise IK re-handshake over a fresh WS + fresh relay grant is at least 2 RTT (grant issuance) + 2 RTT (Noise IK) + cold-wake if the AccountDO is hibernating; at keepalive cadence this could be a steady tax. (2) More importantly: phase-2's effect ledger keys on `effect_id = (origin_device_pubkey, incarnation_uuid, seq)`. The incarnation_uuid is minted "whenever the origin's send-log db is created (fresh install, db loss, restore-from-backup)" — a reconnect does NOT mint a new incarnation (good), but the serving-side high-water fence is per-(pubkey, incarnation). If a reconnect mid-effect reaches the same serving fed-module (it does, via pinned static key), recovery reconciliation re-queries correctly. BUT the claim "cheap" masks the real risk: if reconnect-per-idle-teardown happens at keepalive cadence (Finding 3 + open question 3), every reconnect is a full re-handshake, and the recovery reconciliation path (phase-2 6.1: "recovery queries the SERVING ledger") is exercised on EVERY idle teardown — turning a routine transport event into a ledger-reconciliation event. That is not "cheap."
- **Inheritance verdict**: phase-2 does NOT support this claim.
- **Concrete fix**: Replace "cheap" with a measured budget: "reconnect cost = relay_grant_issuance (1 RTT via AccountDO, +cold-wake if hibernating) + Noise IK re-handshake (2 RTT over the new WS). At keepalive cadence K and idle timeout T, expected reconnects per hour = 3600/T; budget the reconciliation path accordingly." Specify that a relay-pipe reconnect does NOT trigger recovery reconciliation unless the keepalive reaper actually declares a partition (ties to Finding 3).

## Finding 5: Signaling messages have NO replay protection (no nonce/timestamp)
- **Severity**: HIGH
- **Section**: 5.3 (lines 146-154)
- **Confidence**: high
- **Problem**: The signaling ops (`connect_request`, `connect_offer`, `connect_accept`, `relay_open`, `relay_grant`) are JSON over the control WS, "versioned `rdv-v1`." None carry a nonce, timestamp, or sequence number. Phase-2's anti-replay is at the Noise FRAME level (phase-2 line 59: "per-frame integrity + anti-replay") — that protects the data plane, NOT the signaling plane. A compromised AccountDO (or a stolen device token holder) can replay a captured `relay_open {to: pubkey}` to force the relay to mint pipe grants repeatedly (relay-grant flood / resource exhaustion), or replay a `connect_offer {from: pubkey, candidates}` with stale/poisoned candidates to redirect a dial to an attacker-controlled endpoint (the Noise handshake will fail, but it burns the ~2s candidate timeout and degrades to relay — a DoS that also funnels traffic through the relay the attacker controls). `registry_delta` pushes are also replayable — a replayed "device removed" delta could cause peers to unpair prematurely.
- **Concrete fix**: Add a monotonically-increasing `seq` to every signaling message, tracked per-(device, control-WS-session) in the AccountDO; reject any message with seq ≤ last-seen for that device. Add a short-window timestamp (±60s skew budget) to `relay_open`/`relay_grant` so a captured grant cannot be redeemed after TTL. Bind `pipe_token` to a single `relay_open` nonce so a replayed open mints a distinct (rejected-as-duplicate) grant.

## Finding 6: pipe_token semantics are contradictory and enforcement is unspecified
- **Severity**: HIGH
- **Section**: 5.3 (line 153),  (line 200)
- **Confidence**: high
- **Problem**: 5.3 says `relay_grant {relay_url, pipe_id, pipe_token}` is "issued to both sides."  says "per-side `pipe_token`, single-use, short TTL." These contradict: is it ONE symmetric token sent to both sides, or TWO per-side tokens? The security properties differ drastically:
  - If symmetric (one token to both): a stolen device token holder who intercepts the grant can redeem the pipe_token from a third device and MITM-position the relay (the relay authenticates the token, not the device — does the relay also check the device's Noise static key? unspecified).
  - If per-side (two tokens): the relay must bind each token to a specific device pubkey and refuse a connection presenting device X's token from a socket claiming device Y. This binding is NOT specified.
  - "Single-use" enforcement location is unspecified: is it the RelayDO marking the token consumed on first WS upgrade? What if two WS connections race to redeem the same token (TOCTOU before the DO marks it consumed)?
  - Cross-device consumption: can device X redeem device Y's pipe_token? Nothing says no. This is the exact escalation path the threat model ( claims is blocked.
- **Concrete fix**: Specify per-side tokens: `relay_grant → {relay_url, pipe_id, tokens: {initiator: {token, device_pubkey}, responder: {token, device_pubkey}}}`. RelayDO, on WS upgrade, requires the connecting device to present its token AND prove possession of the corresponding device static key (a Noise IK handshake to the relay, or a signed challenge — the relay already sees the Noise static key in the data-plane handshake, so bind the token to that key). Single-use = RelayDO atomically marks the token consumed inside its `webSocketUpgrade` handler (DOs are single-threaded, so no TOCTOU). TTL enforced server-side via DO alarm; a token past TTL is rejected. Explicitly state: device X cannot redeem device Y's token.

## Finding 7: Enrollment race — two devices enrolling the same pubkey; re-enrollment after removal
- **Severity**: HIGH
- **Section**: 4.2 (lines 82-98)
- **Confidence**: high
- **Problem**: 4.2 step 3 says the AccountDO "records the device and mints a device token bound to (account_id, device_pubkey)." Two races are unspecified:
  1. **Same-pubkey concurrent enrollment**: two `POST /v1/device/enroll` with the same `device_pubkey` (e.g. a restored backup, or an attacker who exfiltrated a pubkey — not the private key — trying to shadow-enroll). Does the DO upsert (last-writer-wins, minting a new token that invalidates the old device's token)? Reject? The doc says nothing. If last-writer-wins, an attacker with only the public key can DoS the real device by re-enrolling and rotating its token.
  2. **Re-enrollment after removal**: 4.2 says removal flips the peer's profile entry to `revoked_by_account` "non-routable, requires re-pair if re-enrolled." But `revoked_by_account` is a NEW state that appears NOWHERE in phase-2 (grep confirms: only in phase-3 line 97). Phase-2's profile states are `verified:true|false` + TOFU pinning. How does `revoked_by_account` interact with the existing verified-gate? If a device is removed then re-enrolled with the SAME pubkey, does the peer's pinned key still match (so it silently becomes routable again, defeating the "requires re-pair" promise)? Or does removal unpublish the pin (so re-enrollment presents as first-contact, non-routable until re-verify)? The doc asserts "requires re-pair" but specifies no mechanism to enforce it given phase-2's pinning model.
- **Inheritance verdict**: `revoked_by_account` is NOT a phase-2 state; the interaction with phase-2 TOFU pinning is unspecified.
- **Concrete fix**: (1) Enrollment must be idempotent per (account_id, device_pubkey): a second enroll for an already-enrolled pubkey is rejected with `device_already_enrolled` unless presented with a signed rotation proof (old key signs the new enrollment) — same ceremony as phase-2 key rotation. (2) Define `revoked_by_account` as a tombstone that UNPINS the key on all peers: removal broadcasts a signed revocation; peers mark the pin `revoked` and treat any future presentation of that pubkey as first-contact (non-routable, verify-code required), regardless of prior verification. Re-enrollment of the same pubkey does NOT restore the old pin.

## Finding 8: RelayDO idle-teardown produces false-positive partitions → spurious GOODBYE / OutcomeUnknown
- **Severity**: HIGH
- **Section**:  (line 203),  (line 195), interaction with phase-2 6.2
- **Confidence**: high
- **Problem**:  "idle timeout tears the pipe down (peers reconnect via a fresh grant)." Phase-2 6.2: the keepalive reaper "on declaring a peer partitioned closes that peer's loopback connections → deterministic GOODBYE → OutcomeUnknown for in-flight." The relay pipe idle-teardown severs the WS data path. From the reaper's viewpoint, keepalives stop arriving (the pipe is gone) — indistinguishable from a true peer partition. If the reaper's 3× window fires BEFORE the peers reconnect via a fresh grant, the reaper declares partition → closes loopback → GOODBYE → in-flight effects become `OutcomeUnknown` even though the peer is healthy and the effect may have been delivered. This is a correctness bug that corrupts the effect classification (an effect marked `OutcomeUnknown` that was actually executed forces the caller into the "wait or surface" branch of the recovery table unnecessarily, and if the caller re-invokes, risks a duplicate — the ledger catches the duplicate, but the caller's workflow is disrupted).
- **Concrete fix**: The reaper must distinguish "relay pipe torn down, reconnect in progress" from "peer silent." Mechanism: on relay pipe teardown, the fed-module immediately initiates a fresh-grant reconnect AND suppresses the reap declaration for a bounded `reconnect_grace` window (e.g. 2× the grant-issuance + Noise-handshake RTT budget). Only if the reconnect fails within `reconnect_grace` does the reaper declare partition. This requires the relay-path reap window to be `3× keepalive + reconnect_grace`, NOT the bare TCP 3× window (ties to Finding 3).

## Finding 9: WS framing — length-prefix vs WS-message-length mismatch handling unspecified
- **Severity**: MEDIUM
- **Section**:  (lines 187-191)
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Problem**:  "one fed record per WS message; the 4-byte length prefix is redundant inside a message framing but kept identical so record parsing is carrier-agnostic and golden vectors hold." Two ambiguities:
  1. What if a WS message carries ≠1 record (zero records, or >1)? The doc asserts "one fed record per WS message" but does not say the receiver MUST reject a message that doesn't contain exactly one record. A malicious or buggy relay could concatenate records into one WS message; the carrier-agnostic parser, fed the 4-byte prefix, would parse the first record and silently ignore trailing bytes (or misframe).
  2. What if the 4-byte length prefix disagrees with the WS message length (prefix says 100 bytes, WS message is 96)? The doc says the prefix is "redundant" — but a redundant field that can disagree is a bug source. Does the receiver trust the prefix (and read past the WS message boundary — impossible) or trust the WS frame (and ignore the prefix — then why keep it)?
- **Concrete fix**: Specify: (a) the receiver MUST assert `ws_message_length == 4 + prefix_length` and close the carrier with a typed framing error on mismatch (never silently truncate); (b) a WS message carrying zero or >1 records is a protocol violation that closes the carrier; (c) the 4-byte prefix is parsed but its value MUST equal `ws_message_length - 4` — it is a consistency check, not a length source. Add a golden-vector test for each mismatch case.

## Finding 10: Device clock assumptions — TTLs, last_seen, token expiry rely on device wall-clock
- **Severity**: MEDIUM
- **Section**: 5.2 (line 136 `last_seen_ms`),  (line 200 "short TTL"), 4.2 (token "long-lived with rotation")
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Problem**: The registry entry carries `last_seen_ms` (device-reported? server-stamped? unspecified — line 136 shows it in the entry but line 142 only says the `public` candidate is "stamped server-side"; `last_seen_ms` provenance is unclear). Relay pipe TTL and device-token rotation/expiry all imply time comparisons. If any of these rely on the DEVICE's wall-clock (e.g. a device deciding its token is expired, or reporting `last_seen_ms` from local time), a skewed or rolled-back clock breaks them: a rolled-back clock could make a stale token appear valid, or make `last_seen` report a future time that suppresses online-detection. Cloudflare Workers/DOs have reliable server-side time, but the device side is uncontrolled.
- **Concrete fix**: All time-sensitive fields MUST be server-stamped by the AccountDO/RelayDO (Cloudflare wall-clock), never device-reported. `last_seen_ms` = DO's timestamp on receipt of `hello`/keepalive, not a device field. Device-token expiry = DO-side TTL alarm, not device-side check. The device never makes trust or TTL decisions on its own clock. Document this explicitly.

## Finding 11: "loud device-join events" asserted but not specified as a mechanism
- **Severity**: MEDIUM
- **Section**: 4.3 (line 110),  (line 220)
- **Confidence**: high
- **Problem**: 4.3 and  both lean on "device-join events are pushed loudly to all enrolled devices" as a security control (it's the mechanism that makes a stolen-account rogue-device enrollment detectable). But the mechanism is only asserted, never specified: is it a `registry_delta` push? Does it require user ACK or just render a notification? What if the user's device is offline at join time — is the event queued and delivered on next `hello`? If a rogue device enrolls and the user dismisses/misses the notification, the rogue device remains in the registry and can receive `connect_offer` — the "loud" control is only effective if the user acts on it. The threat model ( treats this as a meaningful defense; under-specification makes it a paper defense.
- **Concrete fix**: Specify: (a) `registry_delta {kind: device_added, device_pubkey, name, enrolled_at}` pushed to ALL online devices on enrollment; (b) offline devices receive the delta as a backlog on next `hello` (AccountDO retains undelivered deltas for N days); (c) the CK app / fed-cli MUST render an un-dismissible banner until the user explicitly acknowledges each new device; (d) the banner shows the new device's verify-code so the user can compare it against the device they just enrolled (distinguishing "my new device" from "rogue").

## Finding 12: Rendezvous-down fallback to static profiles — present-but-down can hang
- **Severity**: MEDIUM
- **Section**: 5.5 (lines 173-180)
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Problem**: 5.5: "Absent → phase-2 behavior exactly." This is the AIRTIGHT requirement. The absent case is clean. But the PRESENT-but-down case is not fully specified: if `[rendezvous]` is configured and the control WS fails to connect (Worker down, DNS fail, AuthKit outage), what happens? The doc says static profiles "keep working forever" but does not say the fed-module MUST continue to dial static-profile peers while the rendezvous WS is reconnecting. A naive implementation could block all dialing until the control WS reconnects (treating rendezvous as a dependency rather than an enhancement). This bricks an otherwise-static-reachable peer during a cloud outage — violating the "static profiles remain the availability fallback" promise (.
- **Concrete fix**: Specify explicitly: rendezvous WS connection failure is NON-BLOCKING for static-profile peers. The fed-module dials static `addr` candidates independently of control-WS state. Registry-discovered peers are simply unavailable while rendezvous is down (they have no static addr). Add a test: rendezvous Worker killed mid-operation → static-profile peer still reachable, registry-discovered peer cleanly fails with `rendezvous_unreachable` (not a hang).

## Finding 13: DO-per-account hot spot — single AccountDO funnels all signaling + registry for a busy account
- **Severity**: LOW
- **Section**: 5.1 (line 119)
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Problem**: One AccountDO per account holds the live WS per online device AND handles all signaling (connect_request/offer/accept/relay_open) AND registry deltas. DOs are single-threaded (single event loop). A busy account with many devices initiating concurrent pairings/signaling floods serializes through one DO. At tool-RPC volumes this is probably fine (signaling is rare relative to data), but a connect_request storm (Finding 5 replay, or a misbehaving client) saturates the DO and blocks registry deltas for all devices on that account.
- **Concrete fix**: Acceptable for v1 (signaling volume is low). Document the ceiling: one AccountDO handles N concurrent control WS + M signaling ops/sec; if exceeded, split signaling into a separate SignalingDO per account (registry stays in AccountDO). Add a per-device signaling rate limit in the AccountDO (e.g. 10 connect_request/sec/device) to bound the storm.

## Finding 14: WS hibernation cold-wake can blow the ~2s candidate timeout
- **Severity**: MEDIUM
- **Section**: 5.1 (line 121), 5.4 (line 164), open question 5
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Problem**: 5.1: "WS hibernation keeps idle accounts at ~zero cost." 5.4: "short per-candidate timeout (~2s)." When an account's AccountDO is hibernating, the first signaling message (e.g. `connect_request`) must cold-wake the DO. Cloudflare DO cold-wake latency is typically 50-200ms but can spike to >1s under load. If the initiator's `connect_request` → `connect_offer` relay round-trip incurs a cold-wake on the TARGET account's DO, the target may not receive the `connect_offer` until >1s after the initiator sent it — and the initiator's ~2s per-candidate timeout is counting down the whole time. If the target's `connect_accept` returns after the initiator's LAN/public candidate timeout has already fired, the initiator falls to relay unnecessarily, or the dial fails entirely.
- **Concrete fix**: (a) Separate the signaling deadline from the candidate-dial timeout: the `connect_request` → `connect_accept` signaling exchange has its own deadline (e.g. 5s, accounting for cold-wake), and only AFTER `connect_accept` does the per-candidate ~2s dial timeout start. (b) Alternatively, use DO `alarm()` to keep the AccountDO warm during expected-active windows (business hours per timezone) and accept the cold-wake cost only for off-hours first-contact. (c) Measure cold-wake p99 in the 3a-1 Miniflare tests and set the signaling deadline from data.

## Finding 15: Stale candidate list → dial to dead/wrong endpoint during registry flap
- **Severity**: MEDIUM
- **Section**: 5.2 (lines 131-138), 5.4
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Problem**: Candidates are "self-reported, refreshed on connect + change" (line 131). A device whose LAN IP changed (DHCP renewal) but hasn't yet pushed a `registry_delta` has a stale LAN candidate in peers' cached registry. A peer dialing that stale LAN addr either times out (burning 2s) or — worse — reaches a DIFFERENT device that now holds that IP:port (if the port is also reused). The Noise handshake will fail against the wrong device's static key (good — no impersonation), but the 2s burn degrades the dial. The `public` candidate is server-stamped and more stable, but the LAN candidate is entirely device-reported and unverified.
- **Concrete fix**: (a) The dialer treats a Noise-handshake failure on a candidate as immediate-fail (don't wait the full 2s) and advances to the next candidate. (b) Candidates carry a `reported_at` timestamp; the dialer deprioritizes candidates older than a freshness threshold (e.g. 5 min) without a confirming `registry_delta`. (c) On successful connect via any candidate, the dialer reports the winning candidate back to the registry to refresh `last_seen` (server-stamped).

## ANSWERS TO THE FIVE OPEN QUESTIONS

**Q1 — Device-token custody on disk (0600 file next to device key): sufficient for v1, or fold into the credentials vault immediately?**
Fold into the credentials vault immediately. The device private key is ALREADY the highest-value secret on the device and is already 0600-file-custodied (per phase-2's per-device keypair model). The device token is a bearer that grants rendezvous access — stealing it lets an attacker impersonate the device to signaling (Finding 5/6). Co-locating the token with the key in the same 0600 file (or vault entry) means the token's custody is no weaker than the key's, and a single read path protects both. A separate 0600 file is not meaningfully simpler and creates a second stealable artifact. Recommendation: store `device_token` in the same vault entry as the device static key, encrypted-at-rest if the vault supports it, 0600 otherwise. Rotation rewrites the vault entry atomically.

**Q2 — Registry candidates self-reporting: hardening against a malicious sibling device lying about its LAN addr?**
The current answer ("it can only redirect its own inbound dials; Noise pins identity regardless") is correct but incomplete. A malicious sibling CAN lie about its LAN addr to redirect a peer's dial to an attacker-controlled host on the peer's LAN (e.g. a compromised IoT device at 192.168.1.X) — the Noise handshake fails, but the peer burns 2s per poisoned candidate and, if the attacker controls the relay candidate too (via a separate relay-grant flood), can force all traffic through the attacker's relay (metadata exposure, timing analysis). Hardening: (a) the dialer MUST treat Noise-handshake failure as immediate-fail, not full-timeout (Finding 15); (b) the server-stamped `public` candidate is authoritative and cannot be sibling-poisoned; (c) LAN candidates are best-effort and the dialer SHOULD prefer server-stamped candidates when available; (d) log candidate-poisoning patterns (repeated handshake failures on a specific sibling's reported LAN addr) and surface to the operator. No structural hardening needed for v1 beyond the immediate-fail-on-handshake-error rule.

**Q3 — Relay pipe lifetime: per-connection grants vs standing pipe per peer-pair — is reconnect-per-idle-teardown churn acceptable at keepalive cadence?**
Per-connection grants for v1, BUT raise the idle timeout well above the keepalive cadence. The churn is unacceptable ONLY if idle-timeout ≈ keepalive-cadence (reconnect every keepalive). Set idle-timeout = max(10 × keepalive_cadence, 5 min) so a healthy pipe stays up across keepalive gaps and only tears down on genuine idleness. Reconnect-per-teardown is then rare (only after real idle), and the "cheap re-handshake" claim (Finding 4) is only exercised occasionally. Standing-pipe-per-peer-pair is a v2 optimization (avoids grant-issuance RTT on reconnect) but adds per-pair RelayDO lifecycle complexity and cost (a standing DO per active peer-pair across all accounts is a large object count). Recommendation: per-connection grants, idle-timeout = 5 min, measure reconnect frequency in 3b-2 tests, revisit standing-pipe if reconnects > 1/hour/peer.

**Q4 — Should connect_offer require the target to be verified before signaling is relayed (quieter unpaired devices), or is offer-to-unverified needed for the pairing UX?**
Offer-to-unverified is needed for the pairing UX and should stay ALLOWED, but with a rate limit and a server-side gate that the target must OPT IN to receiving unverified offers. Rationale: the verify-code ceremony requires the two devices to exchange candidates BEFORE verification (you need a transport to compare codes over, or at least to display each other's candidates). Requiring pre-verification creates a chicken-and-egg: you can't verify without a connection, and you can't connect without verification. The current "allowed, loud" posture is correct. Hardening: (a) a device MAY set `accept_unverified_offers: false` in its registry entry (default true for new devices, false for established devices) — this lets a mature account go quiet; (b) unverified offers are rate-limited per-initiator (e.g. 1/min) to prevent offer-flood DoS; (c) the target's `connect_offer` notification is un-dismissible until acknowledged (ties to Finding 11). The Noise IK handshake still fails for a rogue device, so offer-to-unverified does NOT escalate routability — it only enables the ceremony.

**Q5 — WS hibernation vs signaling latency: acceptable to pay a cold-wake round-trip on first signal to an idle account?**
Acceptable for v1 IF the signaling deadline is decoupled from the candidate-dial timeout (Finding 14). The cold-wake cost is paid once per idle period (the DO stays warm after first wake). The bug is conflating the cold-wake round-trip with the ~2s candidate timeout — they must be separate deadlines. Recommendation: (a) signaling exchange (`connect_request` → `connect_accept`) has a 5s deadline (accounts for cold-wake p99 + 2 RTT); (b) per-candidate dial timeout (2s) starts ONLY after `connect_accept` is received; (c) measure cold-wake p99 in 3a-1 and tune the signaling deadline from data; (d) if cold-wake p99 > 2s, use DO `alarm()` to warm the AccountDO 5 min before expected-active hours (timezone-aware) as a v1.1 optimization. Acceptable to ship v1 with the cold-wake cost paid on first signal, given the decoupled deadlines.
